# Training for a sport

Confidence tags as in `training-principles.md`. Sport-specific strength and
conditioning research is thinner than general training research; many numbers
come from coaches and are tagged `[coaching]`.

## General model

The app's sport templates are **support programs**: strength, power,
conditioning and injury prevention that sit next to the person's actual
practice of the sport. They do not teach technique.

Every sport template is built from the same blocks:

1. **Needs analysis**: energy systems, movement patterns, common injuries.
2. **Strength base**: 2 full-body sessions a week (squat, hinge, push, pull,
   single-leg, core). Beginners in any sport gain from almost any sensible
   strength program `[coaching]`.
3. **Power**: jumps, throws, sprints, done fresh, low volume, full recovery.
4. **Conditioning** matched to the sport's work-to-rest pattern.
5. **Prevention**: the two or three exercises with the best evidence for that
   sport's typical injuries.
6. **Season phase**: off-season (build), pre-season (convert to power and
   specific conditioning), in-season (maintain with 1-2 short sessions).

Rule: strength and conditioning load must leave the person fresh for sport
practice. In-season volume drops by half or more.

Plyometrics: effective for jump, sprint and change-of-direction performance
across ages and sexes; programmes longer than 7 weeks with more than 12
sessions work better; combining bilateral, unilateral, vertical and horizontal
jumps is most efficient `[verified abstracts]`. Start low (about 40-60 ground
contacts per session for novices, landing mechanics first) `[coaching]`.

## Boxing (and striking sports)

Demands
- Rounds of 2-3 min with 1 min rest, roughly a 3:1 work-to-rest ratio; high
  aerobic contribution with repeated explosive bursts `[coaching; Ruddock et
  al. 2016 review, abstract only]`.
- Punch force comes from the legs and hips through trunk rotation.

Program elements
- Strength: squat/trap-bar deadlift, split squat, press, pull-up/row, loaded
  carries.
- Rotational power: medicine-ball rotational throws, landmine rotations,
  cable/band rotations. A 2026 trial found rotation-focused training improved
  punch impact (especially hooks) more than linear training or boxing alone
  `[verified abstract]`.
- Conditioning: intervals in round format (for example 6 × 3 min hard, 1 min
  rest on bag, rope, bike or shadow boxing); one easy aerobic session a week;
  short sprint intervals close to competition.
- Neck and shoulder-girdle strength: plausible protective value, evidence is
  indirect (from other contact sports) `[secondary]`.
- Prevention: shoulder external rotation, scapular work, wrist and hand care.
- Weight: no rapid weight-cut guidance in the app.

## Tennis (and racket sports)

Demands
- Points last about 5-10 s with 10-20 s rest; work-to-rest about 1:1 to 1:4;
  20 s between points, 90 s at changeovers; matches from one to several hours
  with effective playing time around 20-30 % on clay and 10-15 % on fast
  courts (Fernandez, Mendez-Villanueva, Pluim 2006 and related reviews)
  `[verified]`.
- Repeated short accelerations, decelerations, lateral movement, overhead
  serving.

Program elements
- Strength: single-leg strength, hinge, trunk rotation and anti-rotation,
  pulling.
- Power: lateral bounds, split-step jumps, medicine-ball throws (rotational
  and overhead).
- Conditioning: repeated 5-10 s efforts with 15-25 s rest in sets of 6-10
  minutes; court-movement drills; one longer aerobic session.
- Prevention: shoulder external-rotation and scapular strength (the serving
  shoulder gets strong in internal rotation and tight) `[coaching]`, forearm
  and wrist strength for elbow complaints, hip mobility, calf and ankle work.

## Soccer (football)

Demands
- About 10-12 km per match for outfield players `[recall]`. In professional
  men's matches, high-speed running totals 618-1,001 m and sprinting 153-295 m,
  roughly 7-11 % and 1-3 % of total distance; speed thresholds are not
  standardised (2023 systematic review) `[verified abstract]`.

Program elements
- Strength: squat or split squat, hip thrust/deadlift, Nordic curl, adductor
  (Copenhagen plank), calf, core. Traditional strength training was the most
  effective method for 5 m sprint and squat jump in a 2025 network
  meta-analysis `[secondary]`; in elite players strength training alone may not
  improve sprint and jump `[verified abstract]`.
- Speed: short sprints with full recovery, 1-2 times a week.
- Conditioning: high-intensity intervals (for example 4 × 4 min, or 15 s on /
  15 s off blocks), small-sided games where available.
- Prevention:
  - FIFA 11+ warm-up: reduces football injuries by about 30-39 % in
    meta-analyses (the older FIFA 11 did not) `[verified abstracts]`.
  - Nordic hamstring exercise: programmes including it roughly halve
    hamstring injuries (van Dyk et al. 2019; pooled risk ratio about 0.49 in a
    2020 meta-analysis across interventions) `[verified abstract / secondary]`.
  - Copenhagen adduction exercise: in a cluster-randomised trial of 35
    semi-professional teams, one adductor exercise (3 times a week in
    pre-season, once a week in season) lowered the risk of groin problems by
    41 % (odds ratio 0.59; prevalence 13.5 % vs 21.3 %) (Harøy et al. 2019)
    `[verified abstract]`.

The app ships its own warm-up built on the same components (running drills,
strength, balance, plyometrics, cutting) rather than reproducing FIFA
material.

## Basketball (and jumping/court sports)

- Many jumps, cuts and short sprints; ankle sprains and knee tendon problems
  are common `[recall]`.
- Strength emphasis is useful because players tend to be velocity-dominant
  `[secondary]`; in-season moderate-volume plyometrics still improve jump and
  throw power in adolescents `[secondary]`.
- Program: lower-body strength, landing and deceleration mechanics, calf and
  patellar-tendon loading (slow heavy squats, isometric holds), ankle
  balance work, repeated-sprint conditioning.

## Grappling and MMA

- Mixed energy demands, high isometric and grip load.
- Program: full-body strength with pulling and grip emphasis, neck work,
  interval conditioning in round format, low volume when mat time is high.
  Conditioning in combat sports is about repeatable efforts and pacing
  `[coaching]`.
