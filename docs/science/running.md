# Running and endurance

Confidence tags as in `training-principles.md`.

## Intensity

Three-zone model used in research: Z1 below the first lactate threshold (easy,
conversational), Z2 between thresholds (tempo/threshold), Z3 above the second
threshold (hard intervals). The app's five heart-rate zones map onto it:
zones 1-2 → easy, zones 3-4 → threshold, zone 5 → hard.

What the distribution research shows
- Well-trained endurance athletes do most of their volume easy; both
  pyramidal (easy > threshold > hard) and polarised (easy + hard, little
  threshold) patterns are common, pyramidal slightly more so (Sperlich et al.
  2023 review) `[secondary]`.
- Polarised training improved VO2peak more than other models but was not
  better for other performance measures, mostly in studies under 12 weeks
  (Oliveira et al. 2024) `[verified abstract]`.
- A network meta-analysis of individual data suggests competitive athletes may
  benefit more from polarised and recreational athletes from pyramidal
  distribution (Rosenblat et al. 2024) `[secondary, abstract only]`.
- In recreational runners, polarised beat threshold-heavy training for 10 km
  time (5.0 % vs 3.6 %; Muñoz et al. 2014) `[secondary]`.
- Recreational runners tend to run their easy days too hard `[blog-level]`.

App rule: about 75-85 % of weekly running time easy. Recreational plans are
pyramidal (one threshold session, one optional short-interval session);
advanced plans may polarise in the final weeks before a race.

## Building volume safely

- The classic "10 % per week" rule has weak support; trials found similar
  injury rates for 10 % and much larger weekly increases, with risk rising
  mainly above about 30 % `[secondary]`.
- What matters more is the single session: in 5,205 runners followed for 18
  months, a run more than 10 % longer than the longest run of the previous 30
  days was associated with more overuse injuries; week-to-week change was not
  (Frandsen et al. 2025, BJSM) `[verified abstract]`.

App rules
- Long run grows by at most 10 % over the longest run in the last 30 days
  (minimum step 0.5 km for beginners).
- Every 3-4 weeks, a lighter week (about 70-80 % of volume).
- After a break of more than 2 weeks, restart from about 50-70 % of previous
  volume.
- Add frequency before adding long-run length for beginners.

## Session types

| Type | Description | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Easy run | Conversational pace | RPE 3-4, zones 1-2 |
| Long run | Easy pace, longer duration; up to about 25-30 % of weekly volume `[coaching]` | RPE 3-5 |
| Strides | 4-8 × 15-20 s relaxed fast running, full recovery | Fast but smooth |
| Tempo / threshold | 15-40 min total at "comfortably hard", continuous or in 5-10 min reps | RPE 6-7, zone 4 |
| VO2max intervals | 3-5 min reps at about 90-95 % max HR, equal or slightly shorter jog recovery. 4 × 4 min raised VO2max about 7 % in 8 weeks where continuous running at matched volume did not (Helgerud et al. 2007) `[secondary]` | RPE 8-9 |
| Short repetitions | 200-400 m faster than 5 km pace, generous recovery | RPE 8-9, short |
| Hills | 8-10 × 30-60 s uphill, jog down | RPE 9 |
| Run-walk | Alternating intervals for beginners | Easy |
| Race-pace | Segments at goal pace for the target distance | Specific |

Pace guidance: from a recent time trial or race (Daniels/Riegel style
equivalence) `[recall]`; otherwise by RPE and heart rate. The app stores a
"current 5 km" value and updates it from test runs.

## Beginners

The NHS Couch to 5K structure: nine weeks, three runs a week with a rest day
between, starting at 1 min running / 1.5 min walking and building to 30 min
continuous; repeat any week as needed `[verified: NHS]`. Banzir's "Start
running" template follows this pattern with its own wording and schedule.

## Strength training for runners

High-load strength training and plyometrics improve running economy in middle-
and long-distance runners; plyometrics help most at slower speeds, heavy
lifting at higher speeds and in fitter athletes (Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024,
Sports Medicine) `[verified abstract]`.

App rule: every running plan includes an optional 1-2 day strength add-on:
squat or split squat, hinge, calf raises (straight and bent knee), single-leg
work, core, plus low-dose plyometrics (pogo jumps, skips). Heavy leg work goes
on a hard day or at least 48 h before the next hard run.

Injury-prevention accessories with evidence in field sports: Nordic hamstring
curls (see `sport-specific.md`).

## Distances: plan skeletons

| Goal | Weeks | Runs/week | Key sessions | Peak long run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start running (0 → 30 min) | 9 | 3 | Run-walk | 30 min |
| 5 km finish | 8 | 3 | Easy + long + strides | 6-7 km |
| 5 km faster | 8-10 | 3-5 | Intervals + tempo + long | 10-12 km |
| 10 km | 10 | 3-5 | Tempo + intervals + long | 12-16 km |
| Half marathon | 12 | 3-5 | Tempo + long with race-pace | 18-20 km |
| Marathon | 16-18 | 4-6 | Long run, marathon-pace work, threshold | 30-32 km |
| Base / zone 2 | Open | 2-4 | Easy volume | — |

Taper: reduce volume about 20-50 % over the last 1-3 weeks, keep some
intensity `[recall]`.

## Fuel and hydration for long sessions

See `nutrition-fat-loss.md`. For efforts over about 60-90 min: 30-60 g
carbohydrate per hour `[secondary: ISSN]`.
