# Populations and safety

Confidence tags as in `training-principles.md`.

## Standing disclaimer

Shown once in onboarding and available in Settings:

> Banzir gives general training and nutrition guidance. It is not medical
> advice. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or are unsure whether
> exercise is safe for you, check with a health professional first. Stop and
> seek help if you feel chest pain, faintness, or sharp or worsening pain.

## Pre-exercise screening (onboarding step 5)

Ask (all optional): any heart condition or chest pain with exertion; dizziness
or fainting; a bone or joint problem that exercise could worsen; pregnancy or
recent birth; recent surgery; current injury. A "yes" does not block the app.
It shows the note above, selects the conservative variant of the template and
stores the limit in the profile (and in coach memory).

## Stop signals (player and coach)

Stop the session and seek medical help: chest pain or pressure, fainting,
severe breathlessness that does not settle, sudden severe headache, numbness
or weakness.

Stop the exercise and modify: sharp pain, pain that grows during the set,
joint pain above about 3/10, pain that lingers into the next day. Muscle
soreness 24-72 h after new training is normal.

## Beginners

- Start below capacity: the first two weeks teach movements at 3-4 RIR.
- 2-3 sessions a week is enough; add volume only when sessions feel routine.
- Prefer stable, simple exercises (machines, dumbbells, bodyweight) before
  heavy barbell work if no coach is available.
- Expect fast early progress; the progression engine adds load every session
  until it stalls.

## Advanced and competitive athletes

- Need more volume, more variation in intensity, planned deloads and
  season-aware programming.
- Templates expose periodisation blocks and RIR targets; the builder lets them
  write their own.
- The app should get out of the way: fast logging, full export.

## Older adults (roughly 60+)

NSCA position statement (Fragala et al. 2019): resistance training is safe
and beneficial; 2-3 sessions a week, working up to 1-3 sets of 8-12 reps for
major muscle groups; include power (fast intent) and balance work; machines
are a good start for frail or new exercisers, free weights for the fit;
people with unstable conditions should see a doctor first `[verified
abstract/summary]`.

App variant: longer warm-up, balance drills, sit-to-stand and carries, slower
load steps, optional chair-based alternatives, emphasis on walking.

## Youth (under 18)

Properly designed and supervised resistance training is safe for young people
and can reduce sports injuries (NSCA 2009; international consensus 2014):
technique first, progression based on skill, qualified supervision
`[verified abstract]`.

App policy: under 13, the app is not intended for unsupervised use. Ages 13-17:
bodyweight and light-load templates, no maximal testing, no weight-loss
targets, no supplement guidance, a note recommending adult supervision.

## Pregnancy and postpartum

ACOG: in the absence of complications, exercise in pregnancy is safe and
desirable; aim for at least 20-30 min of moderate activity on most days;
walking, swimming, stationary cycling, modified yoga/pilates and strength
training are appropriate; stop and consult a provider for bleeding or other
warning signs `[secondary]`.

App policy: no dedicated pregnancy template in version 1. If the user reports
pregnancy, show the note, suggest medical guidance, hide fat-loss targets, and
let the coach give only general, conservative guidance.

## Higher body weight

Low-impact conditioning first (walking, cycling, rowing), strength training
from day one, slow run-walk introduction if running is wanted, joint-friendly
exercise choices, protein based on reference weight, modest deficit.

## Common limits and substitutions

| Limit | Avoid / reduce | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Wrist pain | Flat-hand support | Parallettes, fists, dumbbells, neutral grip |
| Shoulder pain overhead | Overhead pressing, dips | Landmine press, incline press, push-ups, more rowing and external rotation |
| Knee pain | Deep knee flexion under load, jumps | Box squat, leg press partial range, step-ups, isometric holds, hamstring/glute work |
| Low-back pain | Heavy axial load, loaded flexion | Goblet/split squats, hip thrust, supported rows, core stability |
| Elbow pain (pulling) | High-volume chin-ups, straight-arm work | Neutral grip, rows, reduced volume, slower progression |

These are training modifications, not treatment.

## Overreaching signals the app watches

Performance down on two or more consecutive sessions, rising effort for the
same work, poor sleep, low mood, persistent soreness, elevated resting heart
rate. Response: suggest an easy week. If combined with weight loss or missed
periods, show the REDs note (see `nutrition-fat-loss.md`).
