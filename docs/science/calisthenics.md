# Calisthenics and skills

Confidence tags as in `training-principles.md`. Much of this field rests on
coaching practice (Overcoming Gravity, gymnastics coaching) more than trials;
those items are tagged `[coaching]`.

## Principles specific to skills

- **Straight-arm vs bent-arm strength.** Planche, front lever, back lever and
  handstand load the elbows and shoulders with locked arms. Connective tissue
  adapts more slowly than muscle, so straight-arm work progresses in small
  steps and is not taken to failure `[coaching]`.
- **Tendon recovery.** Tendon collagen synthesis peaks around 24 h after
  loading and returns to baseline by about 72 h (Miller et al. 2005)
  `[secondary, cited in legacy app]`. Leave about 72 h between heavy
  straight-arm sessions for the same pattern.
- **Tendon stimulus.** Improving tendon properties appears to need high
  intensity, around 70 % of maximal voluntary contraction or more `[secondary:
  Oranchuk]`.
- **Isometrics are angle-specific,** and holds at longer muscle lengths give
  more growth and better carry-over to dynamic strength (Oranchuk et al.
  systematic review) `[verified abstract]`. So train the next progression for
  short holds, and add dynamic work through the range (leans, negatives,
  presses, rows).
- **Skill practice is distributed.** Balance skills such as the handstand
  improve best with frequent, short, fresh practice (motor-learning principle,
  Schmidt & Lee) `[recall]`. Handstand: 5-10 min, 4-6 days a week.
- **Order within a session:** skill/balance → explosive → straight-arm
  strength → bent-arm strength → volume/accessories → core `[coaching]`.
- **Push/pull balance and shoulder care.** Heavy planche and handstand volume
  is all protraction and overhead pushing; include horizontal pulling,
  external rotation and lower-trap work every week `[coaching]`.
- **Wrists.** Warm up wrists before any hand-supported work and strengthen
  them (wrist curls, extensions, push-up-position leans). Use parallettes if
  wrists hurt `[coaching]`.

## Hold and rep standards

Default rule: move to the next progression when you can do the standard for
all sets, with clean form, in two consecutive sessions, pain-free.

Isometric work per session: about 30-60 s total time under tension, in holds
of 5-15 s that stop 1-3 s before form breaks `[coaching]`.

## Progression ladders

### Foundations (entry tests for skill programs)
| Movement | Beginner target before skill work |
|---|---|
| Push-up | 15-20 strict |
| Pull-up | 5-8 strict |
| Dip | 8-10 strict |
| Hollow hold | 45-60 s |
| Plank | 60 s |
| Bodyweight squat | 30 |
| Dead hang | 45-60 s |

### Push-up ladder
Incline → knee → full → diamond → decline → archer → pseudo planche push-up →
one-arm progressions.

### Pull-up ladder
Dead hang → scapular pulls → band-assisted / negatives (5 s) → pull-up →
chest-to-bar → L-sit pull-up → weighted or archer → one-arm progressions.

### Dip ladder
Bench dip → negative → parallel-bar dip → ring dip → weighted dip.

### Squat ladder
Squat → split squat → Bulgarian split squat → assisted pistol → pistol squat;
shrimp squat; Nordic curl and single-leg hip thrust for the posterior chain.

### Handstand
1. Wrist prep, pike hold, wall plank.
2. Chest-to-wall handstand: build to 60 s.
3. Wall shoulder taps, toe pulls (balance from the wall).
4. Kick-up practice, freestanding attempts: build to 10 s, 30 s, 60 s.
5. Shapes (straddle, tuck), press progressions.

Handstand push-up: pike push-up → feet-elevated pike → wall negatives (3-5 s)
→ wall HSPU → deficit HSPU → freestanding.

### Planche
Typical timeline: many months to years; body weight and limb length matter.
1. Planche lean: 30 s with shoulders ahead of wrists.
2. Frog stand → tuck planche: 15-20 s × 3.
3. Advanced tuck (flat back): 10-15 s × 3.
4. Straddle planche: 5-10 s.
5. Full planche.

Support work: pseudo planche push-ups, planche lean → tuck negatives, tuck
planche push-ups, band-assisted holds, scapular protraction push-ups.

### Front lever
1. Dead hang, scapular pulls, hollow body.
2. Tuck front lever: 15-20 s × 3.
3. Advanced tuck (flat back): 10-15 s × 3.
4. One-leg → straddle: 5-10 s.
5. Full front lever.

Support work: front lever raises and negatives, tuck front lever rows, dragon
flags, weighted pull-ups.

### Back lever
Skin the cat → German hang → tuck → advanced tuck → straddle → full. Build
elbow tolerance slowly; supinated grip only after months of preparation.

### Muscle-up
Prerequisites: about 10 strict pull-ups, 10-15 dips, chest-to-bar pull-ups.
False-grip hangs (rings) → explosive high pulls → transition drills (low bar
or bands) → negatives (5-7 s) → strict muscle-up.

### L-sit → V-sit
Supported tuck → one-leg → L-sit 20-30 s → V-sit progressions. Needs hip
flexor compression strength and hamstring flexibility.

### Human flag, one-arm pull-up, one-arm push-up
Advanced add-ons; specify later from the same ladder pattern.

## Weekly structures

| Level | Structure |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 3 full-body days: push, pull, legs, core each day; handstand wall practice as a warm-up. |
| Intermediate | 4 days: straight-arm + push / pull / legs + handstand / mixed skills; or upper-lower. |
| Advanced | 4-6 days organised around one or two priority skills, each trained 2×/week with 72 h between heavy exposures (the legacy PLANCHE OS programme is the reference implementation). |

Limit priority skills to two at a time; maintain the rest with low volume.

## Hybrid with weights

Weighted pull-ups and dips are the simplest strength builders for skills; leg
training with weights is recommended since bodyweight leg progressions cap out
early for strength `[coaching]`.
